    To You, Whom My Heart Has Already Chosen

    I know this is a little awkward. Perhaps you're not ready for something like this. Honestly, I don't even know when you will be, and that's okay. I am not writing this because I expect you to have an answer waiting for me. I'm writing because today, I've finally gathered the courage to say everything I've been keeping trapped inside.

    What I'm about to do could change our friendship forever. It could make things a little awkward between us, or perhaps, just perhaps, take us somewhere neither of us has imagined yet. I understand that. I've thought about it more times than I can count. But some feelings become too beautiful to keep hidden, and I think mine have reached that point.

    I know you've been through things that have changed the way you look at love. I know you've been hurt by people you trusted, disappointed by people you gave chances to, and left with questions that perhaps nobody ever bothered to answer. I know trusting someone again isn't as simple as it sounds. Perhaps a part of you is afraid of experiencing the same things all over again. Perhaps you believe you can never love someone the way you once did.

    And I don't blame you for any of that.

    I won't ask you to forget your past, lower your walls overnight, or pretend that everything you've experienced never happened. I don't want to compete with your past or prove that I'm better than people who hurt you. I simply want the opportunity to show you that not everyone who enters your life intends to leave a scar.

    I don't want to be another person you have to recover from. I'd rather be someone you can breathe around.

    If memory is the brain's way of preserving what matters, I think mine has already made a rather permanent decision about you. Somewhere between our conversations, your little habits, your words, and those ordinary moments I somehow started looking forward to, you became a thought I never had to force myself to remember.

It's about remembering the little things, listening when they have something to say, being patient when they don't, showing up when things aren't convenient, and making someone feel valued even on the most ordinary days.

    I want to take care of you. I want to look out for you. I want to celebrate your achievements, listen to your complaints, laugh at your silly moments, and be there when life doesn't go according to plan. I want to know the versions of you that the rest of the world rarely gets to see: the sleepy you, the childish you, the irritated you, the vulnerable you, and even the days when you don't feel like being particularly lovable.

    I don't need you to be happy all the time. You don't have to pretend around me.
